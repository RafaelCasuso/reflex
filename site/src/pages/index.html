What REFLEX does, in order

REFLEX runs as a hook on every tool call of your agent and asks a local daemon for a decision. The daemon works in a fixed order, and the model is the last step, not the first:

  1. It normalizes the call into one canonical shape, the same for every host, and classifies its side effect: a read, a local write, something destructive, external, financial, a credential, a production system.
  2. It redacts secrets locally. Nothing after this step sees them.
  3. It evaluates your policy: the team's rules, your own, the repository's, and REFLEX's few of its own, with an explicit precedence. Most actions are decided here, deterministically, in well under a millisecond.
  4. Only an action the policy leaves open is assessed by a model, and only if you turned one on and consented to what it is sent. Its answer is evidence; the policy's floors still hold.
  5. The decision is recorded with the rules that matched and, later, with what you and the host did about it.

Beside your host, never in its place

Claude Code ships a permission classifier and a sandbox; Codex has its own approval policy. REFLEX does not replace them. It decides from a policy your team wrote and can read, before any model is asked, across hosts, and keeps the record. A sandbox contains what runs; REFLEX decides what runs. The threat model says plainly what REFLEX is not.

Two hosts, one policy

REFLEX installs as a hook into Claude Code and into Codex and translates each host's events into the same canonical action, so the same policy decides for both. Claude Code's integration is verified against the real binary in the repository's tests; Codex's is built from its documentation and says so until it is verified live. What is verified for each host.

A team's policy, without an account

One person signs the team's policy into a snapshot and publishes it to a URL or a shared file. Every member subscribes once with the public key. From then on each daemon applies the team's rules, a local or project policy cannot weaken a mandatory one, and a snapshot that does not verify is refused and reported, never applied.
